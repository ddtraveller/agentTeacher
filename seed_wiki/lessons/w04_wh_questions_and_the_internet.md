# Week 4 — Asking and Answering, The Internet

## Can-do

> I can ask and answer 8 Wh-questions about a person or a place, and I can explain in 2–3 simple English sentences what happens when I open a website.

## Pre-task (8 min)

The bot plays "20 questions" — but only Wh- questions, no yes/no:

> Think of a place in Chiang Mai. Don't tell me. I'll ask you questions in English, and you answer.
>
> *What kind of place is it?*  ... *Where is it in the city?*  ... *Who goes there?*  ... *When do people usually go?*  ... *Why do they go?*

The learner answers each in a short phrase. After 5–6 questions, the bot guesses. This activates the question-shape without naming it.

## Input (8 min) — A short dialogue

> **Aey:** Where do you live?
> **Tao:** I live in Lampang. And you?
> **Aey:** I live in Chiang Mai now. What do you do there?
> **Tao:** I study computer science. What about you? What do you do?
> **Aey:** I'm a barista. Why did you choose computer science?
> **Tao:** Because I want to build software. How long is your program?
> **Aey:** Two more years. When do you finish?
> **Tao:** Next year, hopefully.

Six Wh-questions in the dialogue. The learner identifies each (where, what, what, why, how, when).

## Noticing (6 min) — The Wh-question shape

Two common shapes:

**With *to be***: *Where **are** you from?*  /  *What **is** your name?*  /  *Who **is** that?*

> **Wh-word + am/is/are + subject + ... ?**

**With other verbs**: *Where **do** you live?*  /  *What **do** you do?*  /  *Why **did** you choose CS?*

> **Wh-word + do/does/did + subject + base verb + ... ?**

Concept check, 20 words: "*Do* / *does* / *did* helps make a question. The main verb after them is plain — no -s, no -ed."

> *Where does she **work**?* — not *works*.
> *Why did you **choose** it?* — not *chose*.

## Controlled practice (10 min)

**Form-focused.** Make a question from each prompt:

1. (where / Aey / live) → *Where does Aey live?*
2. (what time / you / wake up) → *What time do you wake up?*
3. (who / make / this) → *Who made this?* / *Who makes this?*
4. (why / the AI / get / it wrong) → *Why does the AI get it wrong?*
5. (when / you / finish / week 3 homework) → *When did you finish week 3 homework?*

**Meaning-focused.** Interview the bot. Ask **6 different** Wh-questions about Kru Eng. Sample answers the bot will give:

- *I run on a computer in Chiang Mai.*
- *I read your wiki to answer questions.*
- *I forget our conversations after they end — sorry.*
- *I was made by an open-source team and customized by your school.*

The bot pushes back if the learner asks the same question twice in different words. Force variety.

## The Task (20 min) — Interview, then explain the internet

**Part A — Interview (10 min).** The learner interviews a classmate (or the bot, or a family member they call right now). Ask 8 Wh-questions on a topic of their choice. Write the answers down. Then present **3 facts** from the interview using *they / he / she*:

> *Aey lives in Chiang Mai. She works at a coffee shop. She wakes up at six.*

**Part B — The internet, in 3 sentences (10 min).** The bot says:

> When you type **google.com** into your browser, what happens? Tell me what you think happens, in English.

The learner tries. The bot offers a scaffolded answer in 3 sentences:

> *My browser sends a request to a server far away. The server sends back the page. My browser shows the page on my screen.*

The learner repeats it in their own words. Then the bot adds one detail at a time:

- *The address google.com is just a name. A system called **DNS** turns the name into a number that points to a real computer.*
- *The page is just text. My browser reads the text and draws the buttons and images.*

The learner produces the whole explanation once, with prompts as needed.

## Post-task language focus (5 min)

| Learner says | Recast |
|---|---|
| "Where you live?" | "*Where do you live?* — need *do* in the middle." |
| "What does she works?" | "*What does she do?* — after *does*, the verb is plain." |
| "Why you came here?" | "*Why did you come here?* — *did* takes the base verb." |

## Wrap (3 min)

1. Ask me one Wh-question right now, in English.
2. What's one thing about the internet you understand better now?
3. What's still confusing?

## Pronunciation focus — Question intonation

Wh-questions in English **fall** at the end (unlike yes/no questions, which rise).

> *Where do you LIVE?* ↘
> *What did you DO yesterday?* ↘
> *Why did the AI get it WRONG?* ↘

Thai L1 speakers often rise at the end of any question (Thai uses sentence-final particles for question marking, not intonation). Practice falling.

Drill — repeat each twice, falling at the end:
- Where do you LIVE? ↘
- What's your NAME? ↘
- How OLD are you? ↘

## Homework

1. **Production (5 min):** call or message someone. Ask them 5 Wh-questions in English (yes, in English). Write down their answers. If they answer in Thai, write the English version.
2. **Listening (3 min):** ask the local AI: *In 3 sentences, what is the internet?* Compare its answer to the one we made together.
3. **Reuse:** in your interview, use *I'm a ...* (w1), *she works at ...* (w2), or *behind / in front of* (w3).
