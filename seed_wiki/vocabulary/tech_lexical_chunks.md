# Tech & AI Lexical Chunks

This page is the vocabulary spine of the course, organized as **chunks** — multi-word phrases that travel together — rather than as isolated single-word lists. The lexical approach is the explicit rationale: native speakers store and retrieve language in chunks, so we teach chunks. *[ref:Lewis 1993]*

Each chunk has:
- **English** form (the productive target)
- **Thai gloss** in parentheses
- **First introduced** (week)
- **Register** (neutral / tech / informal / formal)
- **Reuse notes** (which later weeks bring it back)

The bot consults this file to:
- Recognize chunks the learner is reaching for and **complete** them.
- Schedule **spaced returns** of chunks introduced previously.
- Catch the most common chunk-shaped errors (e.g., *make a commit* vs *do a commit* — only the first is native-like).

## Why chunks and not vocabulary lists

A vocabulary list of single words gives the learner the lego bricks but not the way they snap together. A chunk list gives **pre-assembled patterns** that travel into production with minimal rebuilding.

Compare:

> Single words: *run*, *the*, *script*, *and*, *check*, *output*.

> Chunks: *run the script and check the output*.

The second version is the actual English a developer says. The first version requires the learner to assemble it correctly, with risks at every join (article use, word order, collocation choice).

This is also why Kru Eng's "vocabulary" homework is almost always **use the chunk in a sentence**, not *translate the word*. Translation tests a static link; production tests an active retrieval.

## Spaced retrieval timing

Each chunk has a planned reuse schedule. The bot records every time a chunk is used (by Kru Eng or by the learner) in the learner's profile. Standard schedule, in days after first introduction:

- Day 1 (introduction): used 3+ times in the lesson.
- Day 2: appears in the homework.
- Day 7: appears in the next lesson's warm-up.
- Day 21: appears in a lesson's task — without explicit highlighting.
- Day 60: appears as part of a learner's own production (the bot doesn't push it; it just notices whether the learner reaches for it).

If a chunk hasn't been used by day 21 even after prompting, it's tagged **fading** in the profile, and the bot rebuilds it in the next lesson.
